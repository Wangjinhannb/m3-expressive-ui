# Reference Video Principles

This file distills the design argument from the user's reference video into reusable rules.

## The video's central claim

"Google style" is not the act of placing Google's four colors on icons. The recognizable family comes from a stable visual language that survives across products and redesigns.

## Reusable principles

### 1. Shared world, different products

Google Maps, Gmail, Photos, Chrome, Pixel UI, and other products can look different in function and composition while still feeling related. Recreate the **grammar**, not an exact layout.

### 2. Four colors are identity anchors, not paint buckets

Blue, red, yellow, and green identify the family, but they do not need to appear as four isolated blocks. In expressive moments, colors can overlap, blend, cross, or transition smoothly.

Use multi-color treatment for:

- Brand graphics.
- Loading and progress.
- AI or generation states.
- Hero illustrations.
- Celebration and onboarding.

Keep routine productivity chrome calmer.

### 3. Geometry is simple and highly recognizable

Favor a small number of strong shapes instead of complicated icon construction. A useful icon or container should remain recognizable at small sizes and in silhouette.

Use:

- Circles.
- Rounded rectangles.
- Arcs.
- Simple wedges and cutouts.
- Clear negative space.

Avoid ornamental detail that does not improve recognition.

### 4. Roundness is a system, not a corner-radius trick

The video emphasizes soft, rounded, clean forms in Pixel and Material 3. The effect comes from the relationship between icons, containers, buttons, grouped controls, and motion.

Do not apply the same large radius to everything. Use different shape roles to communicate hierarchy and state.

### 5. Friendly technology, not childish decoration

The goal is to reduce coldness without losing technical clarity. Use softness, color, and movement to make the interface approachable while preserving precision and legibility.

### 6. Motion connects states

Avoid the feeling that one screen simply "cuts" to another. Let elements rotate, stretch, shift, resize, or morph so the user understands continuity.

Good motion answers at least one question:

- What changed?
- Where did this element come from?
- Where did it go?
- What did my action cause?

### 7. Stable grammar, flexible expression

Logos, seasonal themes, gradients, and individual product treatments can evolve. The family remains recognizable because the deeper rules stay stable: color logic, simple geometry, rounded containment, purposeful motion, and clear hierarchy.
